# our future is looking more like hell than heaven

over the next couple of years, oai, anthropic, meta, google are going to consistently build more and more integrations with tools that we consistently use in order to abstract away the maximum number of people's interactions with the internet. why edit photos or videos manually if they can do it for you? why create presentations, economic models, or documentation if they can create them for you? all of your personal and business information will be able to be viewed and queried at any time through one interface.

in order to achieve economies of scale, humans abstract away problems, specialize and automate; those are a few of our greatest gifts. however, each abstraction, specialization, and automation moves us away from a well-rounded understanding of the world and gradually reduces our confidence and curiosity. it is so slow that we barely feel it happening. but as days, weeks, years, and decades go by, i think we can feel it in our soul. they leave me wanting. wanting something else. wanting the joy of learning and the pride of understanding. i wonder how many others feel that way, and what they would be willing to put on the line to change it.

the difference at the current point in human history is that we have entered a present where nobody understands how some things work and the number of things we don't understand will continue to increase. we've never been here before. we usually understand the things we create. the nytimes recently had an article titled "why one of the world's greatest mathematicians is on a 'war footing'", referencing terence tao. he describes his acceptance of ai as "soul-searching at gunpoint" and argues that "a proof that no human can properly explain should be viewed as incomplete." these are the people we should support, not make obsolete. while the aforementioned companies attack unsolved mathematical problems to prove their intellectual superiority, they write essays and mathematical proofs they cannot understand themselves.

we are intentionally running towards a future where we don't understand. where is the proof that this will be good for anyone? this future is idealized by marc andreessen in his "techno-optimist manifesto", which argues we should do everything possible to accelerate technology, no matter the costs. this is someone who urged society to build more housing because cities were unaffordable and then opposed multi-family housing in his own town. this is someone with a net worth of $1.8 billion, 220 thousand times the median human's wealth. my point is not to disparage him (too much), but to illustrate that the ultra-wealthy will not experience any of the costs of the future they believe in.

fire burns. fire keeps us warm at night. fire lights the dark. fire is dangerous. controlling fire takes understanding, diligence and patience. the same can be said for technology.

we're hanging off the edge of a cliff hoping that we fall into heaven. but you don't fall into heaven. you fall into hell.
